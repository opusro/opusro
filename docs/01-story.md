# 01 · Story

This is the story OPUS tells about itself. It is written so that the website,
the App Store pages, a talk and an email all say the same thing. Everything
else in these documents is a consequence of it.

One rule governs how it is told, and it comes from the owner: **the story is
shown, not stated.** The public never reads our principles as a list. They meet
them as a feeling: delight, comfort, and a revived sense that good things can
still be done with technology. Explicit principles are a liability; they invite
semantics and get used against you. So the reasoning lives in these documents,
and the site carries facts, warmth and the work.

---

## The thesis, in one line

**We make tools for people, not for users.**

This is the one sentence we say out loud. It already exists on opus.ro and it
is right. It is a description of what we make, not a claim about anyone else.

## The thesis, for us

Most software is designed around a business model, and the person is fitted in
afterwards. OPUS works the other way round: start from a person and a real need,
make the tool that serves it completely, and only then ask what honest
arrangement keeps it alive. Sometimes that is nothing. Sometimes a small price,
paid once. Never a subscription, never an account nobody needed, never
attention sold on.

That paragraph is the reasoning. It does not go on the home page. What goes on
the home page is a tool that feels this way when you open it.

## The name

*opus*, noun, Latin: a work; a body of work; a composition.

*opus*, adjective, Romanian: opposite; the other way round.

Both are meant. The work, done the other way round. The dictionary treatment on
the current site (the mark expands into two dictionary entries) is the best
thing on it and survives every redesign. It is the brand explaining itself
without a slogan, which is exactly the register the owner wants.

## Where it comes from

For nearly two decades the founder designed mobile products for other
companies, mostly in the US and Scandinavia. That was an education in how good
software is made and in what it is usually made for. In 2026 OPUS shipped its
first tool of its own, Loop, and the studio stopped being a plan.

The tools come from lived need. Loop exists because meditation stopped being
optional and every app for it was built for someone else's business. Chéri
exists because the founder is bad at remembering who has his books and good at
wanting to give people the right thing. inOras exists because the software a
citizen cannot refuse is the worst software in their life, and nobody treats
that as a design problem.

The personal versions of these stories belong to the founder and are told on
his own time, in his own place. The company version stays at this altitude: the
need was real, the existing tools served someone else, so we made our own.

## The shape of the work

Three tools, three circles, widening. The owner confirmed this is the right way
to see and show the line.

| Circle | Tool | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| You with yourself | **Loop** | Time, sound, silence. A timer for practice. Free. A gateway into not needing an app for your moments of peace or focus. |
| You with your people | **Chéri** | Friends and the things between them. Generosity without keeping score. |
| You with everyone | **inOras** | The individual and the city. The software you cannot opt out of, treated with care. Working name; not public yet. |

A tool that serves one of these circles and keeps the principles (03 ·
Products) belongs. Anything else does not, however profitable.

## How it stays alive

We do not fund the work by building the business model into the tools. The
money comes from three honest places, and a fourth place it goes.

- **The tools are free, or charge once.** Loop is free. Chéri is paid once. If
  a tool offers extras, they are delight (sounds, backgrounds), never
  necessity, and never a nag.
- **The studio is supported by the people who find the work valuable.**
  Patronage funds the time to make tools without a model in them. It never buys
  features or influence. Patrons get the studio's notes, early builds, a place
  to talk with us, and a plain yearly account of where the money went.
- **The studio designs for others.** OPUS works with a small number of startups
  that want products made this way. Real revenue, said plainly.
- **Part of what OPUS earns goes to Make Future.** Make Future
  (makefuture.ro) is a small lab for playing with ideas the future should be
  built on: research, education, community. It is separate from OPUS today and
  is meant to become a non-profit. OPUS is its first funder. The relationship
  is stated publicly once Make Future is real enough to point at.

The sentence for the website: *OPUS is supported by the people who use what we
make. If something here is worth something to you, you can help keep it going.*

## Where this ends

The point is not to be big. The point is to keep making tools that are fully on
the side of the person holding the phone, for as long as that can be sustained,
and to show that it can be. A studio small enough to know every person who
writes in, and honest enough to publish why it made each decision, is the whole
ambition.
