# 01 · Story

This is the story OPUS tells about itself. It is written in the words the
website will use, so that the site, the App Store pages, a talk, and an email
all say the same thing. Everything else in these documents is a consequence of
it.

---

## The thesis, in one line

**We make tools for people, not for users.**

That line already exists on opus.ro. It is the right one. Keep it.

## The thesis, in one paragraph

Most software is designed around a business model, and the person is fitted in
afterwards. The subscription decides what the app nags you about. The
engagement target decides what the feed shows you. The growth plan decides
what you are asked to sign up for. The result is software that is technically
brilliant and quietly not on your side.

OPUS works the other way round. We start from a person and a real need, design
the tool that serves it completely, and only then ask what honest arrangement
can keep it alive. Sometimes that is a small price, paid once. Sometimes it is
nothing. It is never a subscription, never an account you did not need, never
your attention sold on.

## The name

*opus*, noun, Latin: a work; a body of work; a composition.

*opus*, adjective, Romanian: opposite; the other way round.

Both are meant. The work, done the other way round. The dictionary treatment on
the current site (the mark expands into two dictionary entries) is the best
thing on it and it should survive every redesign. It is the brand explaining
itself without a slogan.

## Where it comes from

For nearly two decades the founder designed mobile products for other
companies, mostly in the US and Scandinavia. That was an education in how good
software is made and in what it is usually made for. In 2026 OPUS shipped its
first tool of its own, Loop, and the studio stopped being a plan.

The tools come from lived need. Loop exists because meditation stopped being
optional and every app for it was built for someone else's business. Chéri
exists because the founder is bad at remembering who has his books and good at
wanting to give people the right thing. Agora exists because the software a
citizen cannot refuse is the worst software in their life, and nobody treats
that as a design problem.

The personal versions of these stories, including the family events behind the
meditation practice, belong to the founder and are told on his own time, in his
own place. The company version stays at this altitude: the need was real, the
existing tools served someone else, so we made our own.

## The shape of the work

Three tools, three circles, widening.

| Circle | Tool | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| You with yourself | **Loop** | Time, sound, silence. A timer for practice. |
| You with your people | **Chéri** | Friends and the things between them. Generosity without keeping score. |
| You with everyone | **Agora** | The individual and the city. The software you cannot opt out of, treated with care. |

This is the coherent product line the owner asked for. It was already there in
the tools; it only needed naming. It also tells anyone what OPUS would and
would not make next: a tool that serves one of these circles and keeps the
constitution (03 · Products) belongs; anything else does not, however
profitable.

## How it stays alive

We do not fund the work by building the business model into the tools. That is
the whole point, and it means the money has to come from somewhere honest.

- **The tools charge once, or nothing.** The price on the App Store is what it
  costs to keep the tool alive and cared for. It is stated plainly. It never
  recurs.
- **The studio is supported by the people who find the work valuable.**
  Patronage funds the time to make tools without a model. It never buys
  features, priority or influence over the design law. Patrons get the studio's
  letter, early builds, a name in the thanks if they want one, and an honest
  yearly account of where the money went.
- **The studio designs for others.** OPUS works with a small number of startups
  that want products made this way. That work is real revenue, not a side
  hustle, and it is on the website as plainly as the tools are.

The sentence for the website: *OPUS is supported by the people who use what we
make. If something here is worth something to you, you can help keep it going.*

## Where this ends

The point is not to be big. The point is to keep making tools that are fully on
the side of the person holding the phone, for as long as that can be sustained,
and to show that it can be. A studio that stays small enough to know every
person who writes in, and honest enough to publish why it made each decision,
is the whole ambition.
