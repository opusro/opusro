<p class="preface">Written in January 2026, before Loop shipped, and kept here
as it was. We have since come to prefer letting the tools speak. This stays as
a dated record of what we thought we were setting out to do.</p>

These are the principles we return to when we're stuck, when we disagree, when
the easy path and the right path diverge. They're not rules. They're compass
headings.

## I. Respect attention

Attention is the most valuable thing a person can give you. Treat it
accordingly. Never take more than you need. Never manufacture urgency. Never
exploit psychology for engagement.

## II. Ship less

The feature you don't build can't break, can't confuse, can't bloat. Every
addition should justify its existence against the alternative of *nothing*.
Most of the time, nothing wins.

## III. Charge honestly

If the product is free, the user is the product. We charge money because it
aligns our incentives with our users'. We succeed when they're happy, not when
they're addicted.

## IV. Design for the worst moment

Don't design for the user who's alert, focused, and patient. Design for the
user who's exhausted, anxious, and has three seconds of willpower left. If it
works for them, it works for everyone.

## V. No dark patterns

No trick questions in modals. No pre-checked boxes. No "Are you sure you want
to miss out?" copy. No hiding the unsubscribe button. No guilt. No manipulation.
Ever.

## VI. Own the whole stack

Understand every layer of what you build. Don't outsource your core to a
library you haven't read. Dependencies are fine. Ignorance isn't.

## VII. Prefer deletion to addition

When something isn't working, the first instinct should be to remove, not to
add. Complexity is a ratchet. It only goes one direction unless you actively
fight it.

> Perfection is achieved, not when there is nothing more to add, but when there
> is nothing left to take away.
>
> Antoine de Saint-Exupéry

## VIII. Test on yourself

Use your own products. Daily. In real conditions. Not in a demo environment
with perfect data and good lighting. Use them tired, distracted, in bad
lighting, on a slow connection. That's where the truth lives.

## IX. Write it down

If a decision isn't documented, it wasn't made. It was assumed. Assumptions
rot. Write down *why* you made a choice, not just *what* you chose. Your future
self will thank you.

## X. Stay small

Growth for growth's sake is the ideology of the cancer cell. Stay small enough
that every person on the team knows every product, every user complaint, every
line of code. Scale the impact, not the headcount.

These principles aren't original. Most good ideas aren't. But writing them
down, committing to them publicly, makes them harder to quietly abandon when
the pressure mounts.

And the pressure always mounts.
